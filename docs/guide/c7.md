## Check yourself

These questions test whether you could have written the controller files yourself; answer each from memory, then compare with the answers below.

1. `Controller::step` returns q̈* = A⁻¹(μ − Ȧq̇). Write the two-line derivation that shows ȳ̈ = μ, and say which file adds M, C and G for the rigid body.
1. Ȧq̇ is computed along `System::retract`, which for the rigid body is g Exp_I(hζ) and not the screw motion g exp(hζ̂). Why is any curve with c(0) = q and c′(0) = q̇ allowed, and which term of the Taylor expansion does the choice of curve change?
1. Why is the step size of that difference 1e-5 and not 1e-8? Why central and not forward, when a forward difference would save one projection?
1. Why is the β code in `outputs` under `if constexpr (n == 7)`, while the constructor's `if (n == 7 && redundancy_ == nullptr)` is a plain `if`? What would GCC say if the first were a plain `if`?
1. Inside the template, why must you write `out.A.template topRows<6>()`, while `g.block<3,3>(0,0)` in `src/rigid_body.cpp` needs no `template`?
1. The projection at q fails in the middle of a run. What are `qdd`, `st` and η̂ after `step` returns, and why is each one that way?
1. `step` solves with LU anyway. Why does it also compute σ_min(A) by an SVD, why not use the determinant, and how does the 1e-9 threshold relate to the hardware bound σ_min ≥ 1e-3?
1. A colleague changes the ramp to `qdd = A⁻¹ r (μ − Ȧq̇)`. What acceleration do the outputs get while the arm is already moving during the ramp?
1. Why is e_η wrapped into (−L/2, L/2] but the rate error η̇ − η̇_d is not wrapped? What would happen without the wrap when η_d is one loop ahead?
1. You forget `controller.reset(0.1)` before the first rigid-body run. What happens in the first step, and why is forgetting it before a second run worse?
1. Why does `RigidBody::integrate` keep R in SO(3) for any step size, and what does a naive RK4 on the matrix R do?
1. A `System` builds an `Eigen::MatrixXd` inside `output`. Which no-allocation detector catches it, which one misses it, and in which build type?
1. `TangentialReference::constantVelocity(0.5)` does not allocate, but a lambda capturing three doubles does. Why, and why does `step` still never allocate with the second?
1. What does `WavyCouplingOutput` catch that `CouplingRedundancy` cannot?
1. The rigid body's velocity run deviates from the designed response by 9.51e-4 at Δt = 2 ms. What do you expect at 1 ms, and what do you conclude if you measure 9e-4 there?

### Answers

1. ȳ̇ = A(q)q̇, so ȳ̈ = A q̈ + Ȧq̇. Substituting q̈ = A⁻¹(μ − Ȧq̇) gives ȳ̈ = (μ − Ȧq̇) + Ȧq̇ = μ, provided A is invertible (Proposition 2). The caller applies eq:fb: for the rigid body `src/rigid_body_example.cpp:103` computes u = M q̈* + Cζ (G = 0).
1. The central difference (A(c(h)) − A(c(−h)))/2h equals DA(q)[q̇] + O(h²). The curve's second derivative c″(0) appears only in the h² term of A(c(h)), which is even in h and cancels in the central difference; it changes the size of the O(h²) error, not the limit. In a scratch run at the rigid start, the screw motion and the controller's curve both gave Ȧq̇ within 4e-10 of a sixth-order reference (3.5e-10 and 2.1e-10), and the 20 s closed loops were identical to seven digits.
1. The truncation error falls as h² and the rounding error grows as δ/h, where δ ≈ 1e-14 is the error in each computed A. For these A the sum is smallest between h = 1e-5 and 1e-4 (rigid start: 2.1e-10 at 1e-5; Panda paper start: 1.0e-11 at 1e-5), while h = 1e-8 gives 2.3e-7 for the rigid body. A forward difference has O(h) truncation error: its best result was about 5e-8 for the rigid body and 2.3e-9 for the Panda, some 200 times worse. So one extra projection (about 1.7 µs) buys more than two orders of magnitude.
1. A plain `if` still compiles its body for every n. For n = 6 the β block makes a `Vec7` from a 6-entry row, passes the 4 × 4 pose as a `Vec7` and assigns a 7-entry row into the 6 × 6 A, so `Controller<RigidBody>` would not compile: GCC stops at all three lines with `static assertion failed: YOU_MIXED_MATRICES_OF_DIFFERENT_SIZES`. `if constexpr` discards the branch for n = 6. The constructor's condition only compares a pointer, which compiles for both n, so a plain `if` is fine.
1. `out.A`'s type depends on n, which depends on the template parameter `System`. Until `System` is known, the compiler cannot tell that `topRows` is a member template, so `out.A.topRows<6>()` would parse as a comparison `(out.A.topRows < 6) > ()`. In `src/rigid_body.cpp`, `g` is a `Pose`, a concrete type, so `block<3,3>` is known to be a template.
1. `qdd` is unchanged (it is assigned only at `include/se3pf/controller.hpp:310`, after all checks), so the caller cannot use a half-computed command. `st` is a default `Status` with only `t` set and `ok == false` (reset at `include/se3pf/controller.hpp:248`), so a logger never records stale values. η̂ is unchanged (updated only at `include/se3pf/controller.hpp:313`), so the next attempt starts from the last good prediction.
1. Partial-pivot LU does not report rank loss. A nearly singular A gives huge but finite q̈*, which `allFinite` accepts (`StuckBody` in the tests: σ_min = 9.6e-13, finite LU result). σ_min is the 2-norm distance to the nearest singular matrix, the quantity of Proposition 2; a determinant depends on scale (0.1 I₇ has determinant 1e-7 but is perfectly conditioned). The 1e-9 guards arithmetic only, seven or more orders below the values in practice (0.876 to 1.0 for the rigid body, 0.0474 for the Panda start). The safety margin 1e-3 (`HardwareBounds::sigmaMin`) is checked by the MuJoCo runs and the future driver, not by the controller.
1. With q̈* = A⁻¹r(μ − Ȧq̇), the outputs get ȳ̈ = A q̈* + Ȧq̇ = rμ + (1 − r)Ȧq̇. The extra (1 − r)Ȧq̇ is an acceleration nobody asked for, largest at the start of the ramp. Ramping μ alone (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:300`) gives ȳ̈ = rμ exactly; the test at `tests/test_controller.cpp:730-739` checks A q̈* = −Ȧq̇ when moving at t = 0.
1. η and η_d are points of the circle R/LZ, so their difference is defined only up to multiples of L; the shortest signed arc is the meaningful error. η̇ and η̇_d are rates, numbers on the tangent line, so their difference is an ordinary real number. Without the wrap, a reference one loop ahead (η_d = η + L) would give e_η = −L and drive the body around a full loop to "catch up" with a point it is already at.
1. The constructor sets η̂ = 0, so the first Newton run starts at 0 instead of 0.1. From the rigid start that still converges, in 2 updates instead of 1 (from a guess of 3.0 it takes 7). Before a second run η̂ holds the end of the previous run, possibly far from the new start, and Newton's method may converge to a different root of φ_g (not the closest point) or fail. From a guess of 5.0, for example, it converges in 3 updates to η = 5.614, and the controller would then measure (η, ξ) from the wrong point of the path.
1. Within a step the attitude is R_k exp(φ̂), and RK4 integrates only φ (with φ̇ = J_r(φ)⁻¹ω), p and ζ, which live in vector spaces. The final R = R_k exp(φ̂) is a product of two rotations, so it is a rotation up to rounding for any h. A naive RK4 on R forms weighted sums of matrices, which are not rotations. Over 100 s of free motion it drifted to ‖RᵀR − I‖ = 2.5e-13 at h = 2 ms and 7.4e-10 at h = 10 ms (7.0e-11 after 10 s, so still growing); the exponential coordinates stayed below 4e-14.
1. Eigen allocates dynamic matrices with `malloc`, not `new`, so the counting `operator new` sees 0 calls and misses it. `EIGEN_RUNTIME_NO_MALLOC` with `set_is_malloc_allowed(false)` catches it, but only in a Debug build, because the check is an `eigen_assert` that `NDEBUG` removes. A scratch run of exactly this case aborted in Debug with "heap allocation is forbidden".
1. libstdc++'s `std::function` stores a callable of up to 16 bytes inside itself; one `double` (8 bytes) fits, three (24 bytes) do not and go on the heap. Allocation happens when the `std::function` is constructed or copied. `step` only calls `reference_`, which it owns, and calling never allocates (2000 calls: 0 allocations); the reference was built and moved into the controller before the run.
1. The coupling β = q₃ − q₁ ignores η and dη, so a controller that evaluated β at the Newton guess instead of the projected η, or passed the wrong row of J_Ψ⁻¹J_b as dη, would pass every coupling test. The wavy output adds 0.2 sin(8η), so dβ contains 1.6 cos(8η) dη, which changes row 6 of A by more than 0.3; with the guess 0.12 instead of η = 0.1, a β evaluated at the guess would be off by about 0.02, far above the test's 1e-12.
1. About 4.75e-4: the deviation is a zero-order-hold effect of order Δt, and each halving divides it by 2.000 to 2.003 (1.90e-3, 9.51e-4, 4.75e-4, 2.37e-4 for 4, 2, 1, 0.5 ms). A result of 9e-4 at 1 ms would mean an error that sampling does not explain, a bug in A, Ȧq̇ or u. For scale: dropping Ȧq̇ entirely gives 1.9e-2 in this run, and dropping Cζ from u gives 1.2e-2.
