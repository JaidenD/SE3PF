## One control step, traced

One call of `Controller<PandaArm>::step` evaluates the outputs once at rest and three times when moving, then ends with one SVD and one 7 × 7 LU solve. This section follows that call function by function, counts the calls, times them, and gives the numbers they produce on the paper run of `panda_sim`.

### The call sequence

The list follows a moving step. The first step of the paper run (q = `paperStart()`, q̇ = 0) skips items 20 to 22. Types are the return or output types.

1. `Controller<PandaArm>::step(q, dq, t, dt, qdd, st)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:246`). Clears the status with `st = Status()` and stores t. Returns `bool`.
2. `Controller::outputs(q, etaHat_, out)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:216`). Fills an `OutputState` (η, ξ, β, A, iteration count). Returns `bool`.
3. `PandaArm::output(q, &Jb)` (`src/panda.cpp:34`). Multiplies the seven modified-DH transforms and the 0.107 m flange offset, collecting the spatial Jacobian columns (z_i, p_i × z_i) on the way. Then J_b = Ad(g⁻¹) J_s with `inverse` (`src/se3.cpp:124`) and `Ad` (`src/se3.cpp:144`). Returns `Pose g`, writes `Mat67 Jb`.
4. `Tube::outputs(g, guess, out.eta, out.xi, &out.iterations)` (`src/tube.cpp:283`). Returns `bool`; writes η and ξ only on success.
5. `Tube::newton(g, guess, s, e, iterations)` (`src/tube.cpp:259`). First `Tube::wrap(guess)` (`src/tube.cpp:213`) puts the guess into [0, L).
6. `Tube::projectionCondition(g, s, phi, dphi, e)` (`src/tube.cpp:241`) at the guess. It calls `ConingCircle::pose` (`src/path.cpp:67`) for γ(s), `inverse`, then `Metric::Log` (`src/metric.cpp:54`, which calls `logSO3`, `src/se3.cpp:47`) for e_g. Writes `double phi`, `double dphi`, `Vec6 e`; returns `bool`.
7. Inside the same call: `ConingCircle::velocity` (`src/path.cpp:73`) and `ConingCircle::acceleration` (`src/path.cpp:80`) give ζ_γ and ζ_γ′. `Metric::GammaInv` (`src/metric.cpp:74`) and `Ad(inverse(gErr))` give e_g′, and three calls of `Metric::inner` (`src/metric.cpp:30`) give φ and φ′.
8. First Newton update (`src/tube.cpp:267-270`): fail if |φ′| < 1e-12, else s ← wrap(s − φ/φ′).
9. `Tube::projectionCondition` again at the new s. Now |φ| < 1e-10, so `newton` sets η = s and `*iterations = 1` (`src/tube.cpp:273-277`).
10. `Tube::frame(s, N)` (`src/tube.cpp:223`), which is `PeriodicHermite<Mat65>::eval` (`include/se3pf/spline.hpp:36`) without the derivative. Writes `Mat65 N`.
11. ξ = Nᵀ (W e) with `Metric::flat` (`src/metric.cpp:35`) at `src/tube.cpp:291`, then an `allFinite` check. `Vec5`.
12. `Tube::JPsi(eta, xi)` (`src/tube.cpp:318`). Calls `frame(eta, N, &dN)` (a second spline lookup, now with N′), `Metric::Gamma` (`src/metric.cpp:65`, which calls `Jr` and `expSO3`), `Metric::Exp` (`src/metric.cpp:46`), `Ad(inverse(·))` and `path_.velocity(eta)`. Returns `Mat6`.
13. `JPsi.partialPivLu().solve(Jb)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:226`). One LU factorization of J_Ψ, then seven right-hand sides. Returns `Mat<6, 7> top` = J_Ψ⁻¹ J_b, checked with `allFinite`.
14. `out.A.template topRows<6>() = top` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:229`): rows dη and dξ of A.
15. `redundancy_->evaluate(q, out.eta, deta, out.beta, dbeta)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:235`), a virtual call that lands in `LiftRedundancy::evaluate` (`src/lift.cpp:273`). `deta` is row 0 of `top` as a `Vec7`.
16. `Lift::eval(eta, qs, &dqs)` (`src/lift.cpp:211`), which is `PeriodicHermite<Vec7>::eval`: q*(η) and q*′(η).
17. `KernelFrame::eval(eta, X, &dX)` (`src/lift.cpp:265`), the same spline type: X(η) and X′(η).
18. β = Xᵀ(q − q*) and dβ = X + (X′ᵀ(q − q*) − Xᵀq*′) dη (`src/lift.cpp:279-280`). Back in `outputs`, row 6 of A is dβᵀ (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:236`), then β and dβ are checked for finiteness.
19. Back in `step`: ȳ̇ = A q̇ as `VecN yDot` and ξ̇ = `yDot.segment<5>(1)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:256-257`). Then the exact test `(dq.array() == 0.0).all()` (`:263`) decides whether Ȧq̇ is needed.
20. `PandaArm::retract(q, dq, 1e-5)` (`src/panda.cpp:70`) returns q + h q̇. `outputs(q₊, out.eta, plus)` repeats items 2 to 18, with Newton started at the η just found.
21. `PandaArm::retract(q, dq, -1e-5)` and `outputs(q₋, out.eta, minus)`: items 2 to 18 a third time. The `||` short-circuits, so a failed `plus` skips `minus`.
22. `Adq = ((plus.A - minus.A) * dq) / (2.0 * h)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:269`). `VecN`.
23. `reference_.velocity(t, out.eta, nu, dnuDt, dnuDeta)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:278`) calls the lambda of `TangentialReference::constantVelocity` (`:69`): ν = 0.08 and both derivatives 0. Then e_v = η̇ − ν and μ∥ = ∂_tν + ∂_ην η̇ − k_t e_v (`:280-281`).
24. μ = (μ∥, −K_p ξ − K_d ξ̇, −K_p,β β − K_d,β β̇) (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:292-296`).
25. The ramp factor (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:299`): `rampTime_` is 0 in `simulate`, so r = 1 and `quinticRamp` is not called.
26. `Eigen::JacobiSVD<MatN>(out.A).singularValues().minCoeff()` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:304`). Singular values only, no U or V. The step fails if σ_min < 1e-9 or is NaN.
27. `out.A.partialPivLu().solve(mu - Adq)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:307`), an `allFinite` check, then `qdd = result` (`:310`). On every failure path `qdd` keeps its old value.
28. `tube_.wrap(out.eta + yDot[0] * dt)` (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:313`) becomes `etaHat_`, the start of the next cycle's projection.
29. The status fill (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:315-327`): η, η̇, ξ, ξ̇, β, β̇, r, σ_min and `ok = true`. The iteration count is that of the projection at q, not of q±. Returns `true`.

On failure, `step` returns `false` with `st.t` set and `st.ok == false`. If it failed after item 23 (the SVD or the solve), `st.ref` and `st.eTan` are filled too; every other field keeps its default. `simulate` then stops and records `failureTime` (`src/panda_example.cpp:130-134`).

### The code at the heart of the step

The central difference for Ȧq̇ reuses `outputs` along the curve retract(q, q̇, h):

```cpp
// include/se3pf/controller.hpp:259-270
                // Step 7 (continued): Adot qdot = d/dh A(c(h)) qdot at h = 0 along the curve c(h) = retract(q, qdot,
                // h), by a central difference. Both projections start from the eta just found. At rest
                // Adot qdot = 0, and the two projections are skipped.
                VecN Adq = VecN::Zero();
                if (!(dq.array() == 0.0).all()) {
                    constexpr double h = 1e-5;
                    OutputState plus, minus;
                    if (!outputs(System::retract(q, dq, h), out.eta, plus)
                        || !outputs(System::retract(q, dq, -h), out.eta, minus))
                        return false;
                    Adq = ((plus.A - minus.A) * dq) / (2.0 * h);
                }
```

Newton's method on φ_g always takes at least one update, so the count it reports is the number of updates:

```cpp
// src/tube.cpp:259-281
    bool Tube::newton(const Pose& g, double guess, double& eta, Vec6& e, int* iterations) const
    {
        double s = wrap(guess);
        double phi = 0.0, dphi = 0.0;
        if (!projectionCondition(g, s, phi, dphi, e))
            return false;
        // Always at least one update (Online stage), so the count is the number of updates; started from the
        // prediction of the previous control cycle, one update suffices.
        for (int k = 1; k <= 20; ++k) {
            if (std::abs(dphi) < 1e-12)
                return false;
            s = wrap(s - phi / dphi);
            if (!projectionCondition(g, s, phi, dphi, e))
                return false;
            if (std::abs(phi) < 1e-10) {
                eta = s;
                if (iterations != nullptr)
                    *iterations = k;
                return true;
            }
        }
        return false;
    }
```

Each evaluation of φ_g and φ_g′ needs γ, ζ_γ and ζ_γ′ at s, one Log_I and one Γ⁻¹:

```cpp
// src/tube.cpp:245-256
        const Pose gErr = inverse(path_.pose(s)) * g;  // g_err(s) = gamma(s)^-1 g
        if (!metric_.Log(gErr, e))
            return false;
        const Vec6 z = path_.velocity(s);
        const Vec6 dz = path_.acceleration(s);

        // e_g'(s) = -Gamma(e_g)^-1 Ad_{g_err^-1} zeta_gamma (Online stage), so
        // phi_g' = <e_g', zeta_gamma>_W + <e_g, zeta_gamma'>_W
        const Vec6 de = -(metric_.GammaInv(e) * (Ad(inverse(gErr)) * z));
        phi = metric_.inner(e, z);
        dphi = metric_.inner(de, z) + metric_.inner(e, dz);
        return std::isfinite(phi) && std::isfinite(dphi);
```

Every spline lookup is the same constant-time formula. The node index is k = ⌊x/h⌋, with no search, and the cubic Hermite basis weights the two neighbouring values and derivatives.

```cpp
// include/se3pf/spline.hpp:39-53
                const bool finite = std::isfinite(s);
                double x = finite ? std::fmod(s, L_) : 0.0;  // in (-L, L)
                if (x < 0.0)
                    x += L_;
                int k = static_cast<int>(x / h_);
                if (k >= n_)  // rounding can land exactly on L
                    k = n_ - 1;
                const double t = finite ? x / h_ - k : std::numeric_limits<double>::quiet_NaN();
                const double t2 = t * t, t3 = t2 * t;

                y = (2 * t3 - 3 * t2 + 1) * y_[k] + ((t3 - 2 * t2 + t) * h_) * dy_[k]
                  + (-2 * t3 + 3 * t2) * y_[k + 1] + ((t3 - t2) * h_) * dy_[k + 1];
                if (dy != nullptr)
                    *dy = ((6 * t2 - 6 * t) / h_) * y_[k] + (3 * t2 - 4 * t + 1) * dy_[k]
                        + ((-6 * t2 + 6 * t) / h_) * y_[k + 1] + (3 * t2 - 2 * t) * dy_[k + 1];
```

J_Ψ is assembled column by column from the stored frame, Γ and the path tangent:

```cpp
// src/tube.cpp:320-332
        Mat65 N, dN;
        frame(eta, N, &dN);
        const Vec6 e = N * xi;
        const Mat6 G = metric_.Gamma(e);

        // Differentiating Psi = gamma Exp_I(N xi): moving eta moves gamma with velocity zeta_gamma, seen from the
        // pose Psi through Ad_{Exp_I(N xi)^-1}, and turns N xi at the rate N' xi, which Exp_I maps through Gamma
        // (eq:Gamma). Moving xi changes N xi only:
        //   J_Psi = [ Ad_{Exp_I(N xi)^-1} zeta_gamma + Gamma(N xi) N' xi  |  Gamma(N xi) N ]
        Mat6 J = Mat6::Zero();
        J.col(0) = Ad(inverse(metric_.Exp(e))) * path_.velocity(eta) + G * (dN * xi);
        J.rightCols<5>() = G * N;
        return J;
```

The redundancy row costs two spline lookups and a few dot products:

```cpp
// src/lift.cpp:275-280
        Vec7 qs = Vec7::Zero(), dqs = Vec7::Zero(), X = Vec7::Zero(), dX = Vec7::Zero();
        lift_.eval(eta, qs, &dqs);
        kernel_.eval(eta, X, &dX);
        const Vec7 e = q - qs;
        beta = X.dot(e);                               // eq:beta-flat
        dbeta = X + (dX.dot(e) - X.dot(dqs)) * deta;   // eq:dbeta: q moves directly, and through eta(q)
```

### Time per step

`./build-ci-release/tests "[.timing]" -s` reports 12.1 to 12.3 µs per `Controller<PandaArm>::step` on an idle machine (AMD Ryzen 5 5600G, GCC 11.4, `-O3`), and up to 15.6 µs while other jobs ran. The test steps a moving arm (three projections) 20 000 times from the paper start with a random q̇, calling `reset(0.1)` before each step, and asserts below 100 µs. ### Numerical choices in the step

| Choice | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| central-difference step for Ȧq̇ | h = 1e-5 (`include/se3pf/controller.hpp:264`) | Error O(h²) from truncation plus a rounding term that grows like 1/h. Measured at the t = 1 s state against a Richardson-extrapolated reference (entries up to 0.063; largest entry error): h = 1e-3 gives 2.1e-8, 1e-4 gives 2.2e-10, 1e-5 gives 1.4e-10, 1e-6 gives 1.1e-9, 1e-8 gives 6.5e-7. |
| central, not one-sided | (A(q₊) − A(q₋))/2h | A forward difference (A(q₊) − A(q))/h would save one projection, but its error is O(h). It reaches 5.6e-7 at h = 1e-5 and at best about 5.5e-8 (h = 1e-6), against 1.4e-10. The limit DA(q)[c′(0)] q̇ depends only on c′(0), so any curve with c(0) = q and c′(0) = q̇ gives the same Ȧq̇; the Panda uses the straight line q + h q̇. |
| projections for q± start at η(q) | `out.eta` | η(q±) differs from η(q) by about h η̇ ≈ 1e-6, so one update converges. |
| Newton stop | \|φ\| < 1e-10 | φ is in the units of W (m for the Panda, with 1 rad counted as 0.1 m); 1e-10 is far below the smallest number the paper run reports, the final \|ξ\| = 7.1e-7. |
| Newton fail | \|φ′\| < 1e-12, or 20 updates | Near the path φ′ ≈ −1 (−0.993 at the paper start). At φ′ = 0 the update s − φ/φ′ is undefined. Inside the tube φ′ stays away from 0 near the closest point, so a tiny φ′ means the pose left the tube or the guess is far off. |
| prediction η̂ = η + η̇ Δt | `include/se3pf/controller.hpp:313` | Measured at t = 1 s, with the same counts for both signs of the offset: from a guess off by 1e-4 one update suffices. Off by 1e-3 or 0.01 it takes 2, by 0.05 it takes 3, by 0.1 it takes 4, by 0.2 it takes 5. |
| LU with partial pivoting | `partialPivLu()` | Solves J_Ψ X = J_b and A x = μ − Ȧq̇ without forming an inverse: fewer operations and smaller rounding error. |
| SVD before the solve | σ_min(A) ≥ 1e-9 | LU never reports rank loss; a nearly singular A gives huge but finite q̈*. The SVD measures it. 1e-9 only catches numerical singularity; the hardware bound is 1e-3 (`HardwareBounds::sigmaMin`), and the paper run's smallest value is 0.0474. |
| exact zero test of q̇ | `(dq.array() == 0.0).all()` | At rest Ȧq̇ = 0 exactly, so two projections are saved; any nonzero q̇, however small, takes the general path. |

### Offline: constructing PandaExample

`const PandaExample example;` (`apps/panda_sim.cpp:110`) runs the member constructors in declaration order (`include/se3pf/panda_example.hpp:104-113`). The times come from three scratch runs at `-O3` on an idle machine, which agree to within 5 %. The `[.timing]` test measures the whole constructor at 25 to 27 ms on an idle machine, and 32 to 34 ms while other jobs ran.

1. `metric_(exampleMetric(l))` (`src/panda_example.cpp:16`): checks l > 0, then `Metric::product(l * l, 1.0)`, which validates the product type. Under 1 µs (0.13 µs warm).
2. `R0_(restAttitude())` (`src/panda_example.cpp:24`): the flange attitude at q_r from `PandaArm::output(PandaArm::restPosture(), nullptr)`. Under 1 µs.
3. `path_(R0_, center(), radius, cone, metric_)` (`src/path.cpp:45`): A = exp(0.25 ê₁), w₀ = Aᵀe₃ − e₃, and σ from 16 speed samples. About 3 µs.
4. `tube_(path_, metric_)` (`src/tube.cpp:154`): Gram–Schmidt, 2000 RK4 steps (8000 calls of `transportRate`), H, its SVD and planes. Then 2001 node values and derivatives (2001 more `transportRate` calls) go into the spline. The path's `velocity` runs 10 002 times (the extra call is ζ_γ(0) for Gram–Schmidt) and `acceleration` 10 001 times; 4.2 to 4.3 ms in all. The grid size sets the accuracy: H is orthogonal to 3.5e-11 with 2000 intervals and to 3.5e-7 with 200, a factor 10⁴ for 10 times fewer steps, as fourth-order RK4 predicts. With 150 or 100 intervals the construction already fails the 1e-6 guard at `src/tube.cpp:182`.
5. `lift_(path_, PandaArm::restPosture())` (`src/lift.cpp:142`): 2001 projected-Newton solves, with 11 updates at s = 0 from q_r, 9 typical and 13 at most. Each update costs an FK, a 6 × 6 Cholesky and two solves. Then come closure (1.5e-13), joint margins (smallest 0.3592 rad, joint 4, s = 0.367), σ_min(J_b) at every node by SVD (smallest 0.2070) and the spline. It calls `path.pose` 2001 times; about 19 ms in all.
6. `kernel_(lift_)` (`src/lift.cpp:243`): at each of the 2001 nodes one FK and seven 6 × 6 determinants (`kernelVector`, `src/lift.cpp:91`). The smallest X(s_k)·X(s_k+1) is 0.99999966: no sign flips. About 1.9 ms.
7. `beta_(lift_, kernel_)`: stores two references. Under 0.1 µs.
8. `coupling_()`: an empty object; `CouplingRedundancy` has no data.
9. `paperStart_(start(beta_, xi0(), 0.0))` (`src/panda_example.cpp:46`): guess q*(0.1) from the lift, then `startConfiguration` with `levelSet`, `Tube::Psi` and `constrainedIK`: 5 updates, about 7 to 11 µs.
10. `selfMotionStart_(start(beta_, Vec5::Zero(), selfMotionBeta0))`: 4 updates, about 3.5 µs.

Calling `start` in the initializer list is safe only because `lift_`, `tube_` and `beta_` are declared, and so built, before `paperStart_`. The lift takes about 75 % of the construction time because each node solves a nonlinear problem (about 9 Newton updates with an FK each), while the tube integrates a linear ODE (N₀′ is linear in N₀).

### Functions the trace does not reach

Every other public function runs offline, in the simulators, or only in the tests:

- Tube construction only: `initialFrame`, `transportRate`, `rotationPlanes`, `RotationPlanes::log` and `RotationPlanes::exp` (`src/tube.cpp:23-149`), and `PeriodicHermite::build` (`include/se3pf/spline.hpp:20`).
- Lift and kernel-frame construction only: `projectedNewton` and `sigmaMin` (`src/lift.cpp:47`, `:39`), `kernelVector` (`src/lift.cpp:91`), `PeriodicHermite::centralDifferences` (`include/se3pf/spline.hpp:61`).
