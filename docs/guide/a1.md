## What this layer does

The foundation layer supplies what every later module is built from: SE(3) group operations, the metric W and its maps, the path γ, and a periodic spline. Nothing here knows about the tube, the Panda or the controller. Nothing here runs a solver either: every function is a closed form or a table lookup.

### se3: the group, no metric

se3 holds every operation that needs only the group structure of SE(3). Twists are ordered (ω, v), angular first, as in the paper; Murray–Li–Sastry and libfranka use (v, ω). A velocity is always a body velocity, ζ = (g⁻¹ġ)^∨, so ω = (RᵀṘ)^∨ and v = Rᵀṗ.

None of these functions takes a metric. They are free functions in namespace `se3pf` because they have no state: `hat(w)` depends on `w` and nothing else.

### metric: W and the maps of Example 1

`Metric` owns the 6 × 6 matrix W = diag(w_R I, w_p I) and answers ⟨·,·⟩_W, ‖·‖_W, the flat map W♭ v = Wv, Exp_I, Log_I, Γ, Γ⁻¹ and dist. Only this product type has the closed forms of Example 1, so the constructor throws for any other W. With ℓ = √(w_R / w_p), a rotation by θ counts as ℓθ metres.

The two examples use two metrics. The Panda uses w_R = 0.01, w_p = 1 (ℓ = 0.1 m, built in `src/panda_example.cpp:20`); the rigid body uses W = I (`src/rigid_body_example.cpp:12`).

### path: γ, ζ_γ and ζ_γ′ by arc length

A `Path` answers four questions: L, γ(s), ζ_γ(s) and ζ_γ′(s), with s the arc length in W. `ConingCircle` is the Panda example and `TumblingCircle` the rigid-body example. Both have constant speed σ in every product-type W, so s = σθ and every answer is a closed form in the curve parameter θ.

### spline: periodic storage for N, q* and X

`PeriodicHermite<T>` stores values y_k and derivatives y′_k at n + 1 uniform nodes and returns the C¹ cubic interpolant and its derivative at any real s. It is a class template, so one implementation serves `double`, `Vec7` and `Mat65`. The expensive objects (N(s) of Lemma 1, the lift q*(s), the kernel frame X(s)) are computed once at 2001 nodes (the default 2000 intervals) and then read from these splines at 1 kHz.

### Units

s, L, η, ξ and their rates are in the units of W. With W = diag(ℓ²I, I), one unit is 1 m of translation or 1/ℓ rad of rotation. For the Panda (ℓ = 0.1 m), |ξ| = 0.01 means 1 cm off the path or 0.1 rad (5.7°) of attitude error. In general |ξ|² = w_R θ² + w_p d², with θ and d the attitude and position errors to the closest point γ(η).
